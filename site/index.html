How to Interpret This Knowledge Graph

1. Connectivity ≠ Causality

A visual edge or shortest connection path between two entities represents graph connectivity only. It does not establish causal identification, counterfactual validity, or clinical efficacy.

2. Position ≠ Scientific Similarity

Spherical layout coordinates are deterministic navigation anchors (211 paper versions on the sphere, ontology concepts in the core). Spatial proximity is not a measured similarity score or evidence strength.

3. Claims vs. Demonstration

Author claims (claim), method specifications (specification), implementations (implementation), and evaluations (evaluation) are tracked separately. An evaluation stage does not imply a passed test.

4. Scoped Capability & Readiness

Capability levels (L0–L4) and readiness facets (R0–R8) are independent and scoped to specific models, configurations, or studies. Differing configurations are never collapsed to a single maximum label.

5. Surveys Do Not Inherit Cited Systems

Survey or review papers do not inherit the capabilities, evaluations, or readiness levels of the external systems they cite.

6. Evidence & Verification Provenance

Quote occurrence does not establish entailment. Literal quotations are distinguished from explicitly recorded visual paraphrases and OCR drafts (e.g., image-only paper A20).

7. Negative, Unknown & Unassessed States

absent, unknown, not_reported, not_applicable, not_assessed, and conflicting assessments remain visible and filterable. Canonical annotations and coverage sidecars take precedence over stale registry strings.

8. Scientific vs. kg: Serialization Links

Edges prefixed with kg: (such as kg:subject, kg:feature, kg:evidence) are property-graph export metadata, not scientific ontology predicates.
